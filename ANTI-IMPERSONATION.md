# OpenShelves Anti-Impersonation Policy

**Version 3.1 — Effective: 2026**
**Steward:** OpenShelves Labs (`openshelves-labs`)

This is not a trademark claim over the word "OpenShelves," and it does not restrict good-faith use of similar names. It exists solely to stop bad-faith impersonation of the Project or OpenShelves Labs.

---

## What this policy is, and isn't

OpenShelves Labs is **not** trying to claim exclusive ownership of the word "OpenShelves," prevent unrelated independent use of similar names, or stop anyone from forking, modifying, or building on the Project's code — that's governed separately by `LICENSE`.

This policy exists for one narrow purpose: **to stop anyone from falsely claiming to be, or be affiliated with, authorized by, or endorsed by, the OpenShelves project or OpenShelves Labs, when they are not.** It is an anti-impersonation and anti-fraud policy, not a broad brand-control policy.

If your project happens to share a similar name in good faith, with no attempt to pass itself off as this Project or to deceive users about its origin or affiliation, this policy is not aimed at you and OpenShelves Labs is not asserting a claim against you.

---

## What is prohibited

You may not, in connection with software, a service, a fork, or any related offering:

- Claim or imply that your product **is** the official OpenShelves project when it is not.
- Claim or imply **endorsement, authorization, partnership, or affiliation** with OpenShelves Labs or the official Project when none exists.
- Use the OpenShelves name, logo, or visual identity in a way **designed or reasonably likely to deceive** users, customers, or institutions into believing they are dealing with the official Project, its maintainers, or OpenShelves Labs.
- Operate a hosted service, support channel, or sales process that presents itself as official OpenShelves support, official OpenShelves hosting, or an OpenShelves Labs product, when it is not.
- Use a domain name, app listing, or social account designed to be mistaken for the official Project's.
- Register or list "OpenShelves," or a confusingly close variant, as your own app, package, or product name on an app store, package registry, or marketplace in a way that implies it is the official Project.

## What is explicitly fine

- Forking the code under the terms of `LICENSE` and giving your fork its own identity, even if it mentions "based on OpenShelves" or "fork of OpenShelves" truthfully.
- Building an unrelated, independently-conceived project that happens to share a similar or overlapping name, with no intent or effect of confusing users about affiliation.
- Running your own internal deployment and calling it "OpenShelves," consistent with `LICENSE` (e.g., "Springfield University Library — running OpenShelves").
- Honest comparisons, reviews, commentary, or "powered by" / "compatible with" statements that don't claim official status.
- Using the name in academic papers, talks, documentation, or discussion.
- Operating a commercial fork or competing offering permitted under `LICENSE` — this policy concerns false claims of affiliation, not competition itself.

## How this is enforced

OpenShelves Labs will generally reach out directly and request correction before taking any further action. This policy is meant to be used against clear bad-faith impersonation — not as a tool against good-faith community members, forks, or coincidental naming.

If direct outreach does not resolve a clear case of bad-faith impersonation, OpenShelves Labs may pursue available remedies, including but not limited to platform/marketplace takedown requests, domain dispute processes, and trademark registration where warranted. This policy is currently a community-norms statement rather than an asserted trademark right, and will be read consistently with whatever trademark protection OpenShelves Labs holds at the time, if any.

## Questions

Open a Discussion at `https://github.com/openshelves-labs/openshelves/discussions` or contact the Core Team directly if you're unsure whether your use is covered by this policy — OpenShelves Labs is glad to clarify rather than assume bad faith.
